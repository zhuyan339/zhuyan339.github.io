Yan Zhu

Behavioral Science · Data · Practice

Irvine, California

From understanding behavior to supporting meaningful change.

My path began in psychology and moved through years of studying behavior through data. Behavior, of course, does not live in a spreadsheet. Applied Behavior Analysis added another perspective: working with behavior systematically through observation, direct support, and ongoing adjustment.

Behavior is shaped by biology, personal history, psychological states, social environments, and everyday circumstances. No single factor determines what happens next. I am interested in how to support meaningful behavior as it emerges, persists, and adapts.

Patterns in Motion

The questions became clearer as the view changed.

I began in psychology curious about how people think, feel, and respond. Research offered different ways of observing those experiences; practice eventually brought me closer to behavior as it unfolded.

01

Psychology · Youth & Society · Philosophy

People, before variables.

Psychology was my starting point: a broad curiosity about how people think, feel, and respond to the world around them. Youth & Society and Philosophy widened that curiosity toward development, social context, and human agency.

There were many ways to understand a person, and I was still exploring which questions mattered most to me.

02

Addiction Research Center · PIVOT Lab

Different windows into human experience.

At the Addiction Research Center, I supported research modeling alcohol relapse risk using intensive longitudinal data, where self-report could be considered alongside signals such as location and patterns of social interaction. At the Preventing Interpersonal Violence and Overcoming Trauma (PIVOT) Lab, I supported research on sexual violence, trauma, and related psychological outcomes using surveys and daily reports of participants' experiences.

Together, the two settings widened my sense of what psychological research could observe and how different forms of data could offer different views of experience.

03

Applied Behavior Analysis · Frontline Practice

Then I was standing much closer to the change itself.

As a frontline behavior technician, I worked directly with children while observing the larger process around them: parents, behavior technicians, BCBAs, and related clinical services such as OT and PT. I saw progress unfold differently across children and across teams as support was implemented, coordinated, and adjusted over time.

Behavior was no longer something I encountered only after it became data. I could observe it, respond to it, and participate directly in the process of change.

04

ABA Services · Chengdu

Seeing a service work made me curious about building one.

During my years in ABA, I made an early attempt to develop behavioral services in Chengdu. Moving a service model into a different setting quickly exposed constraints beyond the intervention itself, including clinical capacity, staffing, family participation, economics, cultural expectations, and my own inexperience.

A useful intervention still needs a system that can carry it.

05

Independent Research · Protective Behavioral Strategies

The dataset was familiar. My question was not.

I later returned to PIVOT data for an independent study of protective behavioral strategies around alcohol use. Baseline reports described the strategies participants generally used; daily reports offered a way to examine whether those strategies were reflected in behavior across actual drinking occasions.

If a strategy is already known and available, when does it remain present in behavior, and when does it not?

I began by trying to understand people through psychological experience. Over time, my attention moved toward something more visible and dynamic: behavior itself, the conditions around it, and the possibility of changing what happens next.

03

What Happens Next

I want to understand behavior well enough to change what happens next.

Perfect prediction is not the goal. I am interested in understanding behavior well enough to identify where support can make a meaningful difference, then learning from what happens after we act.

What can we change, design, or provide that makes meaningful behavior more likely to emerge, persist, or adapt in everyday life?

01 · UNDERSTAND

Where can we act?

Study behavior in context across people, histories, environments, and moments, not to explain everything, but to find conditions that may be meaningfully modifiable.

02 · SUPPORT

What actually helps?

Develop and test interventions, environments, tools, or forms of support that make meaningful behavior more achievable in real-world settings.

03 · REFINE

What did the response teach us?

Treat behavioral response as new information. Use it to improve when, where, and how support is delivered, and to keep asking how it can work better.

The tools can change.

Digital systems, wearable technologies, and emerging AI tools interest me when they make behavioral support more timely, accessible, engaging, adaptive, or scalable. The technology is not the destination.

Beyond the Data

Behind every behavioral measure is a person.

Working directly with children and families taught me to notice what behavioral measures do not fully capture: effort, hesitation, trust, context, and the meaning that a small change can have in everyday life.

Some moments become data. Others are simply worth remembering.

05

Unfinished Thoughts

A few questions I keep returning to.

Some questions are useful precisely because I have not finished answering them. These sit somewhere between behavioral science, practice, and philosophy.

Agency & Determinism

How much of what we do is chosen, and how much is shaped?

Biology, history, context, and consequences all matter. I am less interested in settling the debate than in asking what remains possible to change.

Understanding & Changing

When does explanation give us somewhere to act?

Understanding behavior matters to me most when it reveals something that can inform meaningful support or intervention.

Change & Maintenance

Must successful support eventually disappear?

Some valuable changes may become independent. Others may continue to benefit from support. I am interested in what success should mean in each case.

Influence & Choice

When does behavioral influence become manipulation?

Intervention always raises a question about goals: which behaviors are worth changing, preserving, or supporting, and who gets to decide?

I expect some of these questions to change as I do. That is part of why I keep them here.
