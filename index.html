From understanding behavior to supporting meaningful change.

My background brings together psychology, behavioral data science, and applied behavior analysis. Behavior, of course, does not live in a spreadsheet. Working directly with children has brought me closer to how behavior unfolds in everyday life, shaped by experience, context, and consequences.

I am interested in what makes meaningful behavior more likely to occur, how actions and their consequences shape future behavior, and why learning does not always translate into action. My goal is to develop and refine interventions that help meaningful behaviors emerge, persist, and adapt across everyday contexts.

Research Reflection

What can the data tell us—and what might we be missing?

My research experiences have taught me to look beyond a result and ask how it was measured, what it means, and what remains unexplained. Across daily reports, intensive longitudinal data, digital activity records, and independent reanalysis, I have become especially interested in how behavior unfolds across situations and over time.

Research Labs

PIVOT Lab

Looking Beyond a Single Report

Working with survey and daily-diary data on psychological experiences and behavior showed me why repeated observations matter. A single report offers a snapshot; reports collected across days can reveal how experiences and actions vary within the same person.

Yet self-reports are not direct windows into behavior. What people recall, notice, and choose to report also shapes the data. This experience taught me to distinguish changes in what we measure from changes in the experiences we hope to understand.

UW Addiction Research Center

More Data, Better Questions

Working with more than 70,000 intensive longitudinal observations introduced me to the challenges of organizing and evaluating real-world behavioral data. Data quality, timing, and the meaning of each measure mattered as much as the number of records.

Different measures can capture different parts of an experience. Before combining signals or interpreting a pattern, I learned to ask what each measure can support—and what conclusions would go beyond the data.

Capstone Project

Healthy Minds Innovations

The Process Behind the Outcome

An app may record whether someone completed an activity, but completion tells us little about the steps that led there.

Using meditation app and survey data, I applied process mining to explore sequences of user activity. Rather than focusing only on endpoints, I examined how different paths through an app could make patterns of engagement visible.

The experience left me with a useful distinction: a digital action can show engagement with an app, but it does not by itself establish learning or psychological change.

Independent Reanalysis

Protective Behavioral Strategies

Revisiting Familiar Data, Asking Different Questions

An independent reanalysis of daily-diary data from the PIVOT Lab examined how protective drinking strategies were used across 1,105 drinking occasions involving 203 participants. By separating differences between individuals from changes within the same person, the analysis explored how habitual strategy use relates to implementation in real-world situations.

Research Reflection

Although participants who reported more frequent strategy use at baseline generally implemented more strategies during drinking occasions, 44.5% of recorded occasions involved no reported strategy use. This contrast highlights an important distinction: habitual patterns of behavior do not necessarily translate into consistent implementation across situations.

The analysis also revealed that when individuals used more strategies than usual, they tended to have lower odds of heavier drinking, with stronger associations observed at higher consumption thresholds. While these observational findings do not establish causality or confirm differences between thresholds, they raise a question worth investigating:

What conditions shape whether a strategy is implemented, and why might its relationship with outcomes vary with their severity?

05

Open Questions

A few questions I keep returning to.

Some questions are useful precisely because I have not finished answering them. These sit somewhere between behavioral science, practice, and philosophy.

Agency & Determinism

How much of what we do is chosen, and how much is shaped?

Biology, history, context, and consequences all matter. I am less interested in settling the debate than in asking what remains possible to change.

Understanding & Changing

When does explanation give us somewhere to act?

Understanding behavior matters to me most when it reveals something that can inform meaningful support or intervention.

Change & Maintenance

Must successful support eventually disappear?

Some valuable changes may become independent. Others may continue to benefit from support. I am interested in what success should mean in each case.

Influence & Choice

When does behavioral influence become manipulation?

Intervention always raises a question about goals: which behaviors are worth changing, preserving, or supporting, and who gets to decide?

I expect some of these questions to change as I do. That is part of why I keep them here.
