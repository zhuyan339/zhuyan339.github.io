Research Identity

I am interested in how meaningful behavior change can be supported in everyday life.

I keep coming back to a simple observation: what people know, want, or are capable of doing does not always become what they actually do.

Across research, behavioral practice, and attempts to build behavioral services, I have become interested in what happens between intention and sustained action, how support can adapt as people and circumstances change, and how behavioral data can help inform what should happen next.

I am still exploring the populations and settings in which I want to pursue these questions. What has remained consistent is my interest in using behavioral science, data, and real-world intervention to understand how meaningful change can be supported over time.

How the Question Took Shape

Different ways of encountering the same problem

My interests did not begin with a single population or method. They developed as psychology, data, practice, and building each changed the way I understood behavior and intervention.

01
PSYCHOLOGY

I started by asking why behavior differs.

Psychology introduced me to the idea that behavior cannot be understood in isolation. Learning history, development, individual differences, and environment all shape how people respond and change.

02
BEHAVIORAL DATA SCIENCE

Data gave me tools to study behavioral variation.

Graduate training in behavioral data science gave me statistical, computational, and visualization tools to study behavior across people, time, and situations.

Working with longitudinal and complex behavioral data taught me to move from observing variation to asking how it can be measured, modeled, and eventually made useful.

03
PRACTICE

Practice showed me that capability alone does not determine action.

Working directly with children and families showed me that what a person does depends not only on what they are capable of doing, but also on the conditions present when action is required.

Support, opportunity, competing demands, prior experiences, and momentary conditions can all change what happens next.

04
BUILDING

Building made implementation part of the question.

Through Nudge Behavior Data Science LLC, I explored a cross-border behavioral service model with collaborators in China. The experience showed me that an evidence-based intervention cannot simply be transferred from one system to another.

Healthcare infrastructure, cost, culture, public awareness, and implementation capacity all shape whether a model can actually work in practice.

How do we design behavioral support that fits both the people and the systems it is meant to serve?

Research & Data

Questions That Have Shaped My Thinking

My projects span different behavioral domains. Each has given me a different way to think about variation, measurement, implementation, and what behavioral data can tell us next.

Independent Research · 2026–Present

When does having a strategy become using it?

Using daily behavioral data, I am studying protective behavioral strategies during real-world drinking occasions. The project distinguishes between having access to a strategy and actually implementing it when it matters.

What it made me ask next What conditions help turn behavioral capacity into action?

R · Longitudinal Data · Multilevel Models · Ordinal Models

Addiction Research · 2024

What disappears when we reduce behavior to an average?

Working with intensive longitudinal alcohol-use data showed me how much behavior can vary within the same person across days and situations.

What it made me ask next If behavior changes within the same person, can support change with it?

Ecological Momentary Assessment · Intensive Longitudinal Data

Graduate Capstone · 2023

Why does access not guarantee engagement?

Using process mining, I studied engagement with a digital mindfulness intervention. The project showed me that making a resource available is different from creating the conditions under which people actually use it.

What it made me ask next Can behavioral systems learn from patterns of engagement and respond differently over time?

Python · Process Mining · Digital Behavioral Data

Collaborative Research · 2023–2024

How does measurement shape the behavior we think we see?

Working with behavioral and survey data taught me how many analytical decisions occur between lived behavior and the variables that eventually enter a statistical model.

What it made me ask next How do we preserve what matters about behavior while turning it into usable data?

R · Data Cleaning · Statistical Modeling · Behavioral Measurement

How I Think

From Behavior to Better Decisions

Data become valuable when they help us decide what to do next.

A completed action, a missed goal, a repeated pattern, or a change in engagement can all become information about what may need to change. I am interested in turning that accumulated behavioral information into a feedback loop for better support.

01 Behavior

What is happening?

Observe behavior across people, situations, and time.

→
02 Data

What can we learn?

Identify patterns, variation, and trajectories.

→
03 Decision

What should happen next?

Identify meaningful and potentially modifiable conditions.

→
04 Support

What can we change?

Adjust the plan, environment, timing, prompts, or resources.

→
05 New Behavior

What happened next?

Measure again. Learn again. Adapt again.

What works, for whom, when, and what should happen next?

The goal is not simply to predict behavior more accurately. It is to use what we learn to make the next decision more useful.

Human-Centered Practice

The Human Side of Behavior Change

Behind every behavioral measure is a person.

Working directly with children and families taught me that behavioral change is experienced before it is measured. Data can capture progress, but not always the confidence, hesitation, effort, trust, and connection that develop alongside it.

Those experiences remind me that behavioral research should remain connected to the people expected to live with its outcomes.

Little Steps
From first learning to trace letters to making it all the way from A to Z in less than two weeks. A small milestone we were happy to celebrate together.
Choosing to Try
Watching hesitation turn into curiosity, and curiosity into a choice to try. A small reminder that approaching something once avoided can begin simply with feeling ready to try.
The Human Side
Data can capture progress, but not everything that grows along the way: the trust, the connection, and the little ways we become part of each other's lives.
What I Hope to Build Toward

Support that can learn from behavior

I want to understand behavior well enough to support change that can live outside the research setting. People differ, circumstances change, and goals evolve. I am interested in behavioral support that can learn from those differences rather than deliver the same intervention to everyone.

My long-term interest is in bringing together behavioral science, longitudinal data, and adaptive intervention to support personally meaningful goals across areas such as health, learning, treatment, and personal development.

Adaptive Support

From prompting behavior to learning from behavior

Digital environments already show how timing, defaults, prompts, feedback, and friction can influence behavior. I am interested in what becomes possible when behavioral principles are combined with longitudinal data and emerging technologies to create support that can adapt over time.

AI makes this especially interesting because it may make continuous, personalized behavioral support more scalable.

Clarify a meaningful goal
Develop an actionable plan
Support implementation
Monitor what happens
Learn from accumulated data
Adapt the support
Looking Further Ahead

What happens beyond the behavior itself?

I am also interested in how sustained behavioral change interacts with psychological and physiological adaptation, and potentially with neural plasticity.

I see this as a direction I want to grow into through further scientific training rather than an area in which I already claim specialized expertise.

The specific population or technology may change. The underlying interest is more stable: how can we understand change well enough to make useful, responsible support possible in everyday life?

The goal belongs to the person. The science helps make change more achievable.

The way I hope to work

Understand behavior carefully.

Learn from the data it generates.

Use that knowledge to build support that works in real life.

I am not yet committed to a single population, technology, or intervention. I want those specific questions to grow from the science, the people, the problems worth solving, and the collaborators with whom I have the opportunity to work.
