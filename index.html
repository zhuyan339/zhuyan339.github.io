Irvine, California

From understanding behavior to supporting meaningful change.

My path began in psychology and moved through years of studying behavior through data. Behavior, of course, does not live in a spreadsheet. Applied Behavior Analysis added another perspective: working with behavior systematically through observation, direct support, and ongoing adjustment.

Behavior is shaped by biology, personal history, psychological states, social environments, and everyday circumstances. No single factor determines what happens next. I am interested in how to support meaningful behavior as it emerge, persist, and adapt.

Research Questions

I am interested in the gap between capacity and action: why behavior changes across situations, what shapes those changes, and which conditions may be modifiable.

When and under what conditions does existing behavioral capacity translate into action in everyday life?

VARIATION

Why does behavior change across people and moments?

Behavior reflects interacting histories, environments, individual differences, and current conditions. I am especially interested in what changes within the same person across situations and over time.

IMPLEMENTATION

When does being able to do something become doing it?

Having a skill, strategy, or resource does not guarantee that it will be used. I study the conditions associated with whether existing capacities are expressed when and where they matter.

MEASUREMENT

How can behavioral data capture meaningful change?

Longitudinal and real-world behavioral data make it possible to study variation that averages can hide. How behavior is measured shapes which patterns we are able to see.

INTERVENTION

Which conditions can be changed?

Understanding behavior becomes most useful when it helps identify modifiable conditions and informs support that can respond to people and contexts as they change.

Research Approach

I approach behavior as something that changes across people, moments, environments, and histories. That makes variation part of the signal, not simply noise.

01 · Observe

When does behavior change?

Observe behavior across people, moments, situations, and real-world conditions.

→

02 · Model

What differs between those moments?

Model within-person variation and examine individual, contextual, and momentary factors associated with behavioral change.

→

03 · Test

Which conditions actually matter?

Test which factors are meaningfully associated with action and which may be modifiable through behavioral support.

→

04 · Adapt

What should change next?

Use behavioral response as new information to refine when, where, and how support may be most useful.

From Practice

Behind every behavioral measure is a person.

Working directly with children and families taught me to notice what behavioral measures do not fully capture: effort, hesitation, trust, context, and the meaning that a small change can have in everyday life.

It keeps my research grounded in a simple principle: behavioral evidence should eventually be useful to the people and settings from which it comes.

Current Directions

From understanding behavioral variation to adaptive intervention.

I want to develop methods for identifying when existing capacities are unlikely to translate into action, which modifiable conditions contribute to those moments, and when support may be most useful.

Observe behavior Detect meaningful variation Identify modifiable conditions Inform adaptive support Learn from response

The goal belongs to the person. The science helps make change more achievable.

Research should eventually return to real life.

I want my work to move in both directions: from real-world behavior to better scientific questions, and from evidence back to interventions, tools, and systems that people can actually use.
