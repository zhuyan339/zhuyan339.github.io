Irvine, California

I study when and under what conditions what people are capable of doing becomes what they actually do.

I am interested in when behavioral capacity translates into action, what disrupts that process in everyday life, and how behavioral data can help us understand when and where support may be useful.

My work brings together behavioral science, longitudinal data, and applied behavioral practice. Across different populations and settings, I keep returning to the same question: What helps existing capacity become action when and where it matters?

The Question

My interests did not begin with a single population, diagnosis, or research method. The same behavioral problem kept appearing in different forms: what someone is capable of doing does not always become what they actually do.

When and under what conditions does existing behavioral capacity translate into action in everyday life?

PSYCHOLOGY

I started by asking why behavior varies.

Psychology taught me to think about behavior as shaped by interacting histories, environments, individual differences, and changing conditions. That made variation itself interesting: why can different people respond differently to the same situation, and why can the same person behave differently as circumstances change?

BEHAVIORAL DATA SCIENCE

Data gave me a way to study that variation.

Graduate training in behavioral data science gave me tools to study behavior across people, situations, and time. Working with longitudinal behavioral data shifted my attention from average differences between people toward variation within the same person: what changes from one moment or occasion to another, and what those changes may tell us about behavior.

PRACTICE

Practice showed me that capacity does not guarantee action.

Working directly with children and families in applied behavior analysis made that distinction concrete. A person may have learned a skill and still not use it under different demands, environments, levels of support, or momentary conditions.

Behavior is not explained by capacity alone. What comes before an action, what follows it, the surrounding environment, prior learning, and the person's current state can all shape whether an existing skill is expressed in a particular situation.

IMPLEMENTATION

Building made implementation part of the problem.

Trying to develop a behavioral service model pushed me beyond asking whether an intervention could work. I had to consider whether it could operate within the people, resources, culture, costs, and systems expected to sustain it.

Across these experiences, my question became less about whether change is possible and more about what allows existing capacity to become action in real life.

Selected Work

Different populations and behaviors have repeatedly brought me back to the same underlying question: what changes between having a capacity, strategy, or resource and actually using it in a particular moment?

Independent Research · 2026–Present

When does having a strategy become using it?

I am using daily behavioral data to study protective behavioral strategies during real-world drinking occasions. The project began with a distinction I found increasingly important: having access to a strategy is not the same as implementing it when it matters.

Repeated observations within the same individuals allow me to ask not only who differs from whom, but also what changes from one occasion to another.

What it made me ask next:
What conditions help turn behavioral capacity into action, and what changes when implementation succeeds on one occasion but not another?

R · Longitudinal Data · Multilevel Models · Ordinal Models

Addiction Research · 2024

What disappears when we reduce behavior to an average?

Working with intensive longitudinal alcohol-use data showed me how much behavior can vary within the same person across days and situations.

That experience shifted my attention toward moments and contexts rather than assuming that a person's average adequately represents how they behave.

What it made me ask next:
If behavior changes within the same person, can our understanding of when support is useful change with it?

Ecological Momentary Assessment · Intensive Longitudinal Data

Graduate Capstone · 2023

Why does access not guarantee engagement?

I studied patterns of engagement with a digital mindfulness intervention using process-mining methods. The project introduced me to a problem that extends beyond digital interventions: making a resource available is different from creating the conditions under which someone actually uses it.

What it made me ask next:
Can behavioral systems learn from patterns of engagement and respond differently over time?

Python · Process Mining · Digital Behavioral Data

PIVOT Lab · 2023–2024

How does measurement shape the behavior we think we see?

Working with behavioral and survey data made measurement itself part of the question. Analytical decisions sit between lived behavior and the variables that eventually enter a statistical model.

How we represent behavior determines which patterns we can detect and which conclusions we can draw.

What it made me ask next:
How can we preserve meaningful behavioral variation while turning it into data we can actually use?

R · Data Cleaning · Statistical Modeling · Behavioral Measurement

How I Study It

I am interested not only in whether a behavior occurs, but in when it occurs, when it does not, what differs between those moments, and which of those differences may be meaningful and modifiable.

01 · Observe

When does capacity become action?

Observe behavior across people, moments, situations, and changing real-world conditions.

→

02 · Explain

What differs between those moments?

Use behavioral and longitudinal data to examine person-level and context-dependent factors associated with when implementation succeeds or fails.

→

03 · Intervene

What can be changed?

Identify potentially modifiable conditions that may help existing capacities translate into action when and where support is needed.

→

04 · Learn

What happened next?

Observe how behavior responds to support and use that response as new information for understanding what may work, for whom, and under which conditions.

What works, for whom, when—and what should happen next?

For me, this is where behavioral science and data science become most useful together. The goal is not simply to predict behavior more accurately. It is to understand behavioral variation well enough to make the next decision more useful.

From Practice

Behind every behavioral measure is a person.

Working directly with children and families taught me to pay attention to what behavioral measures do not fully capture: effort, hesitation, trust, context, and the meaning that a small change can have in everyday life.

Those experiences continue to remind me that behavioral research should ultimately return to the people expected to live with its conclusions.

Where It Leads

From understanding implementation to supporting it.

My long-term goal is to understand when and why existing behavioral capacities fail to translate into action, identify potentially modifiable factors associated with those moments, and use that information to inform adaptive behavioral support.

Intensive longitudinal data make it possible to study behavior as it changes within the same person. I am interested in how those data can help us understand not only who a person is, but also what is happening at a particular moment and which conditions may be shaping whether action occurs.

From variation to intervention

This creates a path from observing real-world behavioral variation to understanding which conditions may be modifiable and eventually using that information to inform more adaptive support.

Observe behavior Identify variation Understand context Identify modifiable factors Inform support Learn from response

The goal belongs to the person. The science helps make change more achievable.

The form that support takes may differ across populations and settings. What interests me is the underlying problem: how behavioral science, longitudinal data, and intervention can work together to support action under changing real-world conditions.

Research should eventually return to real life.

I want my work to move in both directions: from real-world behavior to better scientific questions, and from research back to interventions and systems that people can actually use.

I am less committed to a single population than to a way of asking questions: understand when capacity becomes action, learn from when it does not, and use that knowledge to make the next decision more useful.
