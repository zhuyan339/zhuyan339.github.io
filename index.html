I am interested in understanding how behavioral strategies and interventions translate into behavior in everyday life. My work draws on behavioral science, longitudinal data, and quantitative methods to examine how individual and contextual factors shape behavioral implementation and outcomes.

My research experience spans intensive longitudinal behavioral data, digital intervention engagement, behavioral health, and applied behavioral intervention. From Behavioral Repertoire to Real-World Implementation

One question that increasingly connects my research is why possessing a behavioral strategy, skill, or intervention plan does not necessarily mean that it will be implemented in everyday life.

I am interested in the processes that bridge behavioral repertoire and real-world action: when people use strategies they already know, what disrupts implementation, and which individual or contextual conditions support more consistent behavior.

Behavior in Context

My applied experience has also made me interested in how behavior and intervention unfold across different environments. Intervention does not occur in isolation: families, caregivers, practitioners, routines, and service environments can all shape how behavioral strategies are implemented and maintained.

I am particularly interested in studying these contextual processes without assuming that observations from practice are themselves research findings. Instead, I see applied experience as a source of questions that can be tested systematically.

Quantitative Behavioral Science

My methodological interests center on using longitudinal and repeated-measures behavioral data to study variation both between people and within the same person across situations and time.

I am especially interested in combining behavioral theory with multilevel modeling, within-person analysis, computational methods, and reproducible data workflows to study behavior as it occurs in real-world contexts.

Independent Research · 2026–Present

From Repertoire to Implementation

Question: How does an individual's existing repertoire of protective behavioral strategies translate into strategy implementation during real-world drinking occasions?

Approach: Secondary analysis of longitudinal daily-diary data using person-mean decomposition, mixed-effects modeling, Poisson and COM-Poisson models, ordinal and binary outcome models, diagnostic checks, and sensitivity analyses in R.

Current findings: Stronger baseline protective behavioral strategy repertoire consistently predicted greater real-world implementation. In contrast, day-level elevations in impulsivity and psychological distress did not consistently explain implementation.

Greater occasion-level implementation was also associated with lower-risk alcohol outcomes, including lower levels of alcohol consumption and lower blackout likelihood.

What this led me to ask: Under what individual and contextual conditions does behavioral repertoire translate into implementation in everyday life?

Graduate Research · 2024

Addiction Research Center

At the University of Wisconsin–Madison, I worked with more than 70,000 ecological momentary assessment observations from 336 participants in research on behavioral predictors of alcohol relapse.

My work included exploratory data analysis, data-quality validation, behavioral pattern exploration, and reproducible HTML reporting in R to support the development and interpretation of an alcohol-relapse prediction model.

This experience strengthened my interest in using intensive longitudinal data to study behavior as it changes across time and real-world situations.

Graduate Data Science Capstone · 2023

Digital Intervention Engagement

For my graduate data science capstone with Healthy Minds Innovations, I analyzed more than 5GB of application and survey data to investigate patterns of engagement with a digital mindfulness intervention.

I cleaned and structured complex behavioral data and used process-mining methods in Python with PM4Py to characterize user engagement pathways.

I also examined associations between meditation engagement—including frequency and duration—and mental health and well-being outcomes using statistical and predictive modeling.

Future Questions

My current experiences have led me toward a broader set of questions about behavioral implementation and intervention in everyday environments.

How do behavioral strategies move from knowledge or repertoire into real-world action? Why does implementation vary within the same person across situations? Which individual, interpersonal, and environmental conditions support or disrupt implementation?

I am also interested in how these questions extend to behavioral interventions: how implementation quality, caregiver and practitioner behavior, feedback, and contextual support influence whether intervention effects generalize and persist outside structured settings.

Long-Term Direction

I hope to develop a research program that connects behavioral science, quantitative methods, and applied intervention to better understand behavior in the environments where people actually live, learn, and receive support.

My long-term goal is to contribute research and data-informed tools that help evidence-based behavioral strategies and interventions become more responsive, sustainable, and effective across real-world health, educational, family, and community contexts.
