Research Questions

My interests center on the gap between what people can do and what they actually do under changing real-world conditions.

When and under what conditions does existing behavioral capacity translate into action?

VARIATION

Why does behavior change across people, moments, and contexts?

Behavior reflects more than ability alone. Learning history, environment, individual differences, and momentary conditions can shape whether a behavior occurs in a particular situation.

MEASUREMENT

What can within-person variation tell us?

Longitudinal behavioral data make it possible to move beyond averages and examine what changes within the same person across days, situations, and decisions.

IMPLEMENTATION

What turns capacity into action?

Having a skill, strategy, resource, or opportunity does not guarantee that it will be used. I am interested in the conditions that help explain when implementation succeeds and when it breaks down.

INTERVENTION

Which conditions can we change?

Understanding behavioral variation becomes most useful when it helps identify modifiable conditions and informs support that can respond to people and contexts over time.

Research Approach

I approach behavior as something that changes across people, moments, environments, and conditions—and use that variation as information.

01 · Observe

When does behavior change?

Observe behavior across people, moments, situations, and real-world conditions.

→

02 · Explain

What differs between those moments?

Examine person-level, contextual, and momentary factors associated with behavioral variation.

→

03 · Intervene

What can be changed?

Identify modifiable conditions that may help support action when and where it matters.

→

04 · Learn

What happened next?

Use behavioral response as new information about what may work, for whom, and under which conditions.

What works, for whom, when—and what should happen next?

From Practice

Behind every behavioral measure is a person.

Working directly with children and families taught me to notice what behavioral measures do not always fully capture: effort, hesitation, trust, context, and the significance of small changes in everyday life.

It keeps my research grounded in a simple principle: measurement matters most when it helps us understand and support behavior in the settings where people live.

Current Directions

Modifiable Conditions

Adaptive Support

Exploring how behavioral data can inform support that responds to when, where, and for whom it is useful.
